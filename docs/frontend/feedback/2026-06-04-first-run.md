# First-run walkthrough — 2026-06-04

A tester (not the primary audience) narrated their first-ever session with the
site cold, step by step. Captured here because a fresh first-run mental model is
invisible once you've built the thing. Participants anonymised: **T** = tester,
**D** = dev response in-thread.

This is a *capture*, not a plan. Several items D already knew about or had
planned; those are marked so we don't re-litigate. The new signal is the
**onboarding friction** — the moments T hesitated or guessed wrong.

---

## The walkthrough (T's narration)

1. **Lands on the timeline.** First impression: *"am I somewhere wrong? is this
   not the homepage?"* The timeline-as-landing read as "I've ended up somewhere
   deep", not "this is the front door". Led T to hit the **home icon** top-left
   looking for an actual homepage.

2. **Home icon jumped to *tomorrow*.** Clicking home moved the date to Fri Jun 5
   when it was still Jun 4 (~18:55 local). *"not really 5th of june here lol."*
   T rationalised it as "after daily reset I guess."

3. **Settled on a mental model by elimination:** *"okay, it must be a
   planner-focused site so that's what it has."* — i.e. the site never *told*
   them what it was; they inferred it.

4. **Studied a banner card, hit the symbols.**
   - **Carats** — guessed correctly: *"must be what I get when it comes around,
     since missions and such also have them, okay."*
   - **Dice symbol** — total blank: *"can't really make anything out of it and
     don't know where to get an explanation about it."* No affordance pointing
     to an explanation.

5. **Discovered cards can be added to the planner.** Opening the planner: *"kinda
   ambiguous, but should be the cards' release and reruns."* — another inference,
   not a told fact.

6. **Read the planner row columns** (TOTAL / GIFT / DAILY / TICKET / CARAT).
   - *"19 tickets should be how many tickets I'll get until the rerun I guess —
     but then what's the carat value?"* The relationship between the columns
     wasn't legible.
   - **`338` carats** read as *"unnecessarily specific"* — false precision; the
     exactness undermined trust rather than building it.

7. **Removing a planned item was undiscoverable.** *"I now understand how to
   remove those... but took me a while to figure out."* Before finding it, T
   *"tried right-clicking, dragging, or looked for an x mark in the planner
   first"* — i.e. reached for every standard remove affordance and found none.

---

## Already known / fixed (D)

- **Date jump on home (item 2).** Known bug. First version treats the server
  reset (16:00 UTC / UK) such that it jumps the date. **Already fixed properly**
  — the broken state existed because this build was for testing interaction.

## Already planned (D) — feedback confirms direction

- **Carats come off the cards (item 4).** The little per-card carats are story
  rewards on *new original* banners; they clutter the view. They'll be **removed
  from the card face but still tracked**. (Confirms T's read that they were
  noise — note T *did* guess their meaning, but agreed they're clutter.)
- **Dice → pulls, expanded on the banner (items 4, 6).** The dice are pulls.
  Plan: **break them out on the banner by type** — free carats, paid carats,
  tickets, etc. — *all expressed in units of pulls*. This directly answers T's
  "what's the carat value / how do columns relate" confusion.
- **Planner stops being a *planning* tool (items 5, 6, 7).** The planner page
  **stays as a plan viewer** but is no longer where you commit — the add/remove
  *interaction* moves onto the banner itself:
  - a **`+` / `−` on the banner** adds / removes a pity (200 pulls);
  - it **goes red when you can't afford it**, and the line on the scrubber
    **drops below the bar and turns red**.
  This dissolves the "how do I remove a planned item" problem (item 7) — you
  commit/uncommit in-context on the banner, not in a separate page — and replaces
  the abstract column table with an in-context affordability signal. The planner
  page survives as a read-only view of the resulting plan.

## New actionable polish (not yet covered)

These are the findings without an existing fix/plan attached — the raw
onboarding-friction signal:

- **The site never says what it is (items 1, 3, 5).** T inferred "planner-focused
  site" three separate times by elimination. The timeline-as-landing reads as
  "deep inside an app", not "the front door". *Open question:* a one-line
  what-is-this / first-run hint, a clearer landing identity, or making the
  timeline self-evidently the product. Even the eventual on-banner model assumes
  the user knows they're looking at a planner.
- **No path from a symbol to its explanation (item 4, dice).** Even after the
  dice→pulls redesign, the lesson stands: a glyph with no legend / hover / "?"
  leaves the user stranded with nowhere to look. Worth a general affordance
  (hover legend, or a key) so future symbols don't repeat this.
- **False precision erodes trust (item 6, `338`).** An over-exact number reads as
  *less* credible to a newcomer, not more. Watch this in the on-banner pulls
  readout too — consider rounding / banding, or making the precision obviously
  *yours to plan against* rather than a mysterious forecast.
- **Standard remove-affordances were the first thing tried (item 7).** With
  commitment moving onto the banner, the instinct is still right-click / drag-off
  / an `×`. Whatever the on-banner `−` looks like, it should match one of these
  learned gestures.

## Part 2 — the account/config screen (deep-dive)

The walkthrough continued into the **account screen** (reached by clicking the
balance, top-right). Read with one lens up front (D's framing): a large share of
this half is **domain-knowledge gaps of a non-target user** — what a daily pack
is, that the weekly-login rewards are a fixed cycle, the monthly shop tickets —
*not* UI defects. The primary audience (the spreadsheet crowd) already knows these.
So the bar for "tutorialise it" is low-priority; the bar for "give it a cheap home
for its meaning" (an info circle) is the real, recurring ask. Separated below.

### The intended onboarding mechanism (D)

- **Tazuna, top-right, is the planned guide — dual role (decided 2026-06-04).**
  She is best used as **on-demand help** *and* an **onboarding assist**:
  - *Onboarding assist* — pops up on first visit with tips, and explains the
    account screen on first save.
  - *On-demand help* — a persistent affordance the user can return to at any time
    to ask "what does this mean?", rather than a one-shot intro that's gone after
    dismissal.
  This dual role is also the **preferred home for the recurring "info circle"
  ask** — meanings live with her on demand instead of scattering a tooltip on
  every control. Right now she's a **non-functional placeholder emoji** (to become
  hand-drawn clovers later — needs art). This is the answer to Part 1's "site never
  says what it is" and "symbols have no home for meaning" — *the mechanism exists,
  it's just not wired yet.*
- **Clicking the balance opens the account screen.** This is where the user sets
  their snapshot + config. T: *"that spawned most of my next questions."*

### New actionable (has a plan / cheap win)

- **`Save` gives no feedback.** T entered values, hit save, *"didn't get told
  anything."* D: **make it go green when saved.** (And tazuna should pop up to
  explain on first save.)
- **Input fields read as if they'd recompute on scrub.** T expected the
  balance/account fields to be *calculated* and to change when dragging the
  timeline; they don't (they're your **snapshot input**, not derived). The
  derived-vs-input distinction isn't visually legible. *"so couldn't figure what
  it is."*
- **The dice icon is the wrong glyph (reinforced).** Even after working out
  19 ticket-rolls + 338 carat-rolls = 357 total, T: *"probably should've guessed
  it but the dice icon made it hard."* Confirms Part 1 item 4 — the dice→pulls
  redesign should also reconsider the **glyph**.
- **Recurring ask: a hoverable info circle.** T asked for this *by name* — *"a
  little hoverable info circle or something similar could help, unsure how many
  people know about it."* It surfaced on nearly every config control below. This
  is the single highest-leverage cheap win in the whole interview.

### Domain-knowledge gaps (non-target user; low priority to *teach*, but each wants an info circle)

- **Daily Pack** — *"what is daily pack?"* (it's daily *paid* carats). T: *"just
  my lack of knowledge."*
- **Weekly Login `25—25—25—75`** — *"what is this supposed to even mean?"* The
  stepper is illegible without knowing the login cycle is a **fixed reward
  sequence**; you just pick **which day of the cycle you're on**. T: *"ooooooh I
  didn't know the rewards were always same."*
- **Monthly `Friendship / Silver / Gold / Rainbow` checkboxes** — *"couldn't make
  any sense of."* These are the **monthly shop scout tickets** (Rainbow/Gold/Silver
  Cleat exchanges). D: *"just trying to make it easier to use than the
  spreadsheet."*
- **Target Tier → `Open League`** — *"what does open league mean? is it the other
  league you can pick or just being group B 3rd?"* It's the **Graded League**
  outcome you expect. D: the **sweatiness selector** will replace this manual
  pick for most people.

### Confirms existing plans

- **Sweatiness selector** replaces the manual rank pickers (Team Trial rank, Champ
  Meet target tier, Open League). D: *"just pick how sweaty you are."* — see the
  PvP graded-rewards / sweatiness work (#19).
- **Tickets show on banners; shards not handled yet.** T: *"how to check the
  ticket and shards?"* D: tickets appear on banners; **shards are an open design
  question** (*"I'll have think about how to do that"*).
- **Events notifier** — D will add one (*"when I get to it"*). The config screen
  deliberately mirrors the community spreadsheet so concepts are pre-taught — but
  that only works for users who already know the spreadsheet.

### The positive signal (don't lose this)

Despite the friction, the reaction was strongly positive — *"this is already way
above my expectations,"* *"my first reaction was 'did a club member do this? this
is so cool!'"* The **mascot + polish read as community-made and delightful**, and
the core value (*"calculating every single carat and shard that drops in the
future... super accurate"*) landed once explained. The friction is all at the
**onboarding seam**, not the substance.

---

## One-line takeaway

Almost every friction point was the user **inferring** rather than being
**told** — what the site is, what a symbol means, how to undo, what a config knob
does. Two mechanisms already on the roadmap close most of it: the **on-banner
commitment redesign** (Part 1: planner, dice, removal) and **wiring up tazuna**
(Part 2: onboarding assist *and* on-demand help). The user's literal "info circle"
ask is best **answered through tazuna's on-demand role** — meanings live with her,
not scattered as a tooltip on every control. And critically: the back half is
mostly a **non-target user hitting domain-knowledge gaps** — worth a home for
their meaning, *not* worth building a full tutorial for an audience that isn't the
target. The substance delighted; only the seam needs work.
