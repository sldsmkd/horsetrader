## Cadence-drift watch

`legendrace-014` was the only material timing miss. The type-specific
`DateMapper` extrapolated from its last local segment:

- JP `legendrace-012` → `legendrace-013`: May 10 → June 24, **45 days**.
- Confirmed EN starts for the same pair: May 7 → June 7, **31 days**.
- JP `legendrace-013` → `legendrace-014`: June 24 → September 23, **91 days**.
- Extending the 31/45 local slope projected 63 days after June 7: **August 9
  UTC**, exactly the frozen prediction.
- Cygames announced **August 13 UTC**, four days later.

Those UTC drops are Monday August 10 at 07:00 JST predicted versus Friday
August 14 at 07:00 JST actual. Japan's national holiday calendar places
Mountain Day between them on Tuesday August 11:
<https://www8.cao.go.jp/chosei/shukujitsu/gaiyou.html>.

That calendar alignment is a plausible confounder, but the Legend Race should
not be interpreted in isolation. Re-fitting the shared acceleration from every
confirmed JP+EN event at successive EN-date cutoffs gives:

The fit remains extremely tight, so there is no abrupt cadence break. There is,
however, a small monotonic rise in the fitted slope: the implied Global speedup
has eased from about 1.49× at the first useful cutoff to 1.41× now. On the
current full-history line, confirmed 2026 Q3 events (27 correlations) have a
mean residual of **+3.95 days** and median **+4.33 days** — EN is arriving later
than the constant-slope fit expects. That broader signal is approximately the
size of the Legend Race miss.

Q3 follows Global's anniversary month, however, when a dense release calendar
deliberately warps nearby spacing. The fit counts correlated events, not
independent release days, so co-released anniversary content also gives that
local distortion extra weight. Deduplicating identical JP→EN date pairs does
not remove the displacement (August's eight unique pairs still average about
**+4.7 days**), but it remains an anniversary-adjacent calendar effect rather
than evidence that the long-run cadence has changed. Reassess only after
several ordinary months establish a new line.

Conclusion: retain the linear all-event model because it is still explaining
the schedule exceptionally well, but watch whether the positive recent
residual persists. Mountain Day (and the customary Obon period around the
actual date) is supporting context, not evidence for a Legend-specific rule.

### Working regime hypothesis

The expected long-term shape is piecewise linear rather than perpetual
catch-up: Global continues on the compressed cadence until it is about one year
behind JP, then moves towards slope 1.0 while retaining that offset. A full-year
localisation buffer avoids rushing the work and restores seasonal meaning — a
JP Christmas event can remain a Global Christmas event one year later.

At the current all-event slope (`0.708178`), extending the present line reaches
an exact 365-day offset around JP **2032-08-01** / Global **2033-08-01**. That is
only a projection, not a scheduled breakpoint. Evidence for the transition
would be a sustained upward bend in recent all-event residuals and rolling
slopes across ordinary months; anniversary-adjacent warping and isolated
event-type misses should not trigger it.
