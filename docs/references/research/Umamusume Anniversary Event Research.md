# **Strategic Live-Operations and Player Retention Economics in Umamusume: Pretty Derby (Milestones 0.5 to 5.0)**

## **Executive Analysis of Cygames Anniversary Live-Operations**

The Japanese mobile gaming market is defined by highly competitive live-operations (Live-Ops) models. Within this landscape, Cygames’ *Umamusume: Pretty Derby* serves as a primary case study of player retention, resource pacing, and monetization dynamics. To stabilize daily active users (DAU) and maximize player lifetime value (LTV), the developer utilizes a dual annual cadence of "Anniversary" campaigns. Major anniversaries occur in late February to mark the game's launch, while minor "Half Anniversary" milestones occur in late August.1  
The execution of these campaigns relies on a predictable, multi-phased release structure.3 Major anniversaries historically deploy four distinct phases spanning a six-week period, whereas minor milestones utilize a more condensed three-phase framework.3 This phasing strategy manages player fatigue while introducing targeted monetization "anchors," such as paid select gacha banners and specialized item exchange shops.1

Major Anniversaries (1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0):  
Phase 1 (Countdown) ──\> Phase 2 (Main Event \+ New Scenario) ──\> Phase 3 (Mid-Anniv) ──\> Phase 4 (Late Event)

Minor Anniversaries (0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5, 4.5, 5.0\*):  
Phase 1 (Countdown) ──\> Phase 2 (Main Event \+ New Scenario) ──\> Phase 3 (Consolivation & End Game)  
\*Note: The 5.0 Anniversary optimized this sequence by consolidating major operations into a 3-phase cycle.

A core element of this retention loop is the integration of Phase 2 with a new "Training Scenario".2 These scenario releases restructure the game's meta, rendering previous training strategies obsolete and generating high demand for new, scenario-specific support cards.2 To ease the transition for free-to-play (F2P) players during these sudden meta shifts, Cygames deploys "loss-leaders" sent directly to player mailboxes, such as massive jewel presents (typically 3,000 jewels) 7 and free gacha pull campaigns.1  
Additionally, players can earn a fully limit-broken (4凸) promotional SSR support card through a structured "2+3 distribution split." Two copies of the card are awarded via the Phase 2 login bonus, while the remaining three copies are gated behind commemorative missions.7 This design forces players to actively play the newly released training scenario to unlock the card's full utility, transforming a free reward into a driver of gameplay engagement.

## **Half Anniversary (0.5 Milestone) – August 2021**

The 0.5 Half Anniversary established the Live-Ops blueprint for minor, semi-annual milestones. Implemented across three phases, the campaign concentrated late-summer player engagement and re-engaged lapsed players.1  
By launching a countdown phase followed immediately by a massive 3,000-jewel mailbox gift, the campaign stabilized player velocity leading into the autumn live-ops cycle.1

## **Systemic Evolution of Gacha Economics, Progression Gating, and Player Psychology**

An analysis of *Umamusume*'s Live-Ops history reveals several key strategies regarding resource pacing, gating mechanisms, and player psychology:

\[Countdown Phase\] ────\> \[Main Phase Launch\] ────\> \[End Game Consolidation\]  
   1,500 Jewels            3,000 Jewels Mailbox      SSR Exchange Ticket  
   Login Bonus             2x SSR Promo Card         3x SSR Mission Cards  
(Generates Hype)         (Eases Meta Transition)     (Drives Daily Active Play)

### **1\. Retention-Driven Support Card Gating (The 2+3 Split)**

The "2+3 split" for promotional SSR support cards (such as SSR Satono Diamond or SSR Victoire Pisa) is a key mechanism for driving daily active play.2 By awarding the first two copies through the Phase 2 login bonus, Cygames provides players with an immediate reward that encourages them to try out the new training scenario.2  
However, because support cards in *Umamusume* require maximum limit-breaking (4凸) to unlock their high-tier support effects and skill levels, a 0凸 or 1凸 copy is generally not viable in competitive play.  
To unlock the final three copies needed to fully limit-break the card, players must complete a series of commemorative missions tied directly to the new training scenario.7 This gating mechanism ensures that players cannot simply collect their rewards and exit; they must actively play the new content to make their free asset viable, effectively driving daily active play metrics during major campaign windows.

### **2\. Progression Gating of Limit-Break Crystals**

Another notable aspect of *Umamusume*'s economy is the progression loop used for high-value items, such as *Rainbow Crystal Pieces* (虹の結晶片) and *SSR Support Card Exchange Tickets* (SSR引換券).7 While standard jewels and basic currencies are distributed directly to the player's mailbox to drive login metrics, high-value materials are strictly gated behind active gameplay requirements.8  
This gating is primarily achieved through commemorative missions tied to Phase 2 and Phase 3 of the anniversary events.6 For example, the highly sought-after *SSR Support Card Exchange Ticket* is consistently locked behind the completion of all Phase 3 commemorative missions.6  
This ensures that even players who spend heavily cannot bypass active play requirements; players must participate in the latest training scenario to acquire these valuable materials.  
Similarly, *Rainbow Crystal Pieces*—which are needed to assemble *Rainbow Limit Break Crystals*—are placed as milestone rewards in Phase 2 and Phase 3 mission boards.8 This strategy maintains a balanced progression pace:

1. **Phase 1 (Countdown):** Generates early interest and encourages daily logins.6  
2. **Phase 2 (Main Launch):** Introduces a major shift in the gameplay meta.3  
3. **Phase 3 (Consolidation):** Drives deep engagement by rewarding completionists with meta-relevant materials.6

### **3\. Transition from 4-Phase to 3-Phase Operational Cycles**

The transition observed during the 5.0 Anniversary—where a major anniversary consolidated from the traditional four-phase cycle into three phases—reflects a refinement in Cygames' operational pacing.29 Historically, major anniversaries suffered from a "Phase 4 lull" in late March, as player interest dipped once the initial excitement of the new scenario subsided.  
By consolidating the campaign into three phases, Cygames eliminated this downtime, aligning the event's duration with the peak attention span of the player base. This optimized structure allowed the developer to maintain high player velocity throughout the campaign, paving the way for a faster, more natural transition back into the game's regular monthly event loop, such as the highly competitive Champions Meetings.17
